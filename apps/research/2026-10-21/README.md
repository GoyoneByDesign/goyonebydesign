## VIEW4REAL: evidence and practical value

**Previously reviewed candidates, not new discoveries:** the [September 20 neural precompensation study](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/12/9/456), [Nagoya VISAPP binocular study](https://www.scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2026/144351/) and [Pyolimp research code](https://github.com/pyolimp/pyolimp) remain comparison candidates, not integrations. The publisher endpoints returned 429 and 403 respectively during this review; indexed primary abstracts do not substitute for re-reading the full experiments. Pyolimp's repository-level MIT label does not resolve upstream model, weight or dataset rights. No model was downloaded or executed. The [Japanese lab page](https://www.cv.nitech.ac.jp/) corroborates March 9–11 VISAPP activity, not an exact paper publication day or clinical evidence. Chinese-language searches again surfaced special-optics simulation rather than a verified ordinary-screen correction advance.

**New to this review, not newly published:** [Thin Maxwellian Virtual Reality Near-Eye Display](https://sid.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jsid.70065), Kim, Choi and Park, first published **April 3, 2026**. A holographic optical element and mini-Fresnel array reduce part of a point-light-source optical arrangement. This is a physical optical prototype evaluated with camera focus settings, not an ordinary-screen software result. Approximately 0–3.3 D camera-focus tolerance is not a clinically supported prescription range. Reduced effective resolution, grid artifacts and color effects remain limitations. Data are available on request; reusable implementation rights were not established. Decision: background only, with no hardware purchase or integration.

Retain the existing experimental boundary: a shared ordinary screen cannot independently address each eye without suitable optics; output must remain within display brightness and contrast limits. Inverse filtering can amplify noise, ringing and clipping rather than recover unavailable information. Prescription inputs alone do not establish the real point-spread function, accommodation, screen distance, pupil size or worn-correction calibration. No supported prescription range, safety, clinical benefit or glasses replacement is established by this review.

Before changing algorithms, require independently held-out synthetic PSFs and mismatch cases for distance, pupil and interocular differences; compare linear-light bounded output against matched-size normal text, enlargement and sharpening. Report clipping, contrast, artifacts, each eye and worst-eye regressions, Latin/CJK readability, runtime and memory on the target device. Preserve semantic text, zoom/reflow and neutral reset. These are retained evaluation requirements, not tests completed today. No personal prescription, camera or document data were collected.
