# View4Real research review — October 20, 2026

Reviewed at 12:38 UTC (8:38 a.m. EDT). Decision: **no optical-algorithm change or new release justified by this review**. Keep existing experimental functionality unchanged; defer neural-model integration pending reproducible evaluation and licensing review.

English, Japanese and Chinese searches did not establish a new validated ordinary-screen precompensation result beyond previously reviewed candidates. This is a bounded search result, not an exhaustive claim about all research.

## Findings and dates

- **Ordinary-screen precompensation — previously reviewed:** [Dual-Input Neural Networks for Personalized Image Precompensation](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/12/9/456), September 2026 issue. Publisher-indexed text distinguishes display-bounded precompensation from unrestricted deconvolution: negative or out-of-range brightness cannot be displayed. This remains an offline comparison candidate, not a newly discovered result or an integration recommendation. Direct full-text retrieval was unavailable in this review; no new human-validation claim is made.
- **Japanese binocular work — previously reviewed:** [Binocular Visual Distortion Correction on Display, Nagoya Institute of Technology, VISAPP 2026](https://www.scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2026/144351/). Publication year 2026; exact publication day not established here. The indexed primary abstract addresses different left/right visual characteristics. Direct publisher retrieval returned HTTP 403. This review does not independently verify full-text experiments or infer actual reading benefit from the abstract.
- **Chinese special-optics research — classified in this review:** [Proposal of full-color metalens light field displays: a physics-simulation-driven pre-compensation scheme](https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-65-18-6168), Sun Yat-sen University, published **June 12, 2026**. This is not an October publication. The accessible publisher page explicitly describes simulation of a designed metalens array and a display-surrogate/precompensation network pair. Data are available on request rather than publicly provided. It is special-optics simulation, not an ordinary-monitor software result or demonstrated human reading improvement.
- **Commercial headset distinction:** [Apple Vision Pro Digital Prism Correction](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121387), support page published **September 14, 2026**. The page documents per-eye prescription-prism setup for Vision Pro with visionOS 26 or later; its page date does not establish the feature's original release date. A headset-specific prism feature is not evidence that ordinary-screen deconvolution can independently correct both eyes.

## Retained evaluation direction

Before considering a rendering change, compare bounded linear-light output against matched-size normal, enlargement and sharpening baselines; use independent synthetic point-spread-function holdouts and parameter-mismatch cases. Report each eye separately, including worst-eye regressions, rather than hiding them in an average. Include Latin and CJK text. These are evaluation proposals, not tests completed today or demonstrated improvements.

No app code, binary, version, permissions, personal eye data or device settings changed in this research review. No optical-bench, clinical or new human validation ran. Nothing here establishes clinical correction, commercial readiness or replacement of prescribed eyewear.
