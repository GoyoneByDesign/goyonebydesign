Newly identified research relevant to VIEW4REAL

1. Personalized neural precompensation: stronger comparison candidate

Published September 20, 2026; reviewed October 16. Alkzir et al. compare USRNet-PC, DWDN-PC and KerUnc-PC with established methods. The principal contribution for this project is a reproducible benchmark candidate, not a product-ready filter. Their human evaluation used simulated retinal images and participants instructed to use normal or corrected vision; displays were uncalibrated. The authors explicitly describe computational limits and lack of clinical-effectiveness evidence. Their image test set is broader than the current prototype's synthetic examples, but a new independent PSF holdout remains important. Paper, publication date

Decision: investigate an offline, synthetic comparison before any integration. The paper pins Pyolimp revision e3db3bf2897753aa8d27ec8b299621ee6a77dd35; its top-level code license is MIT. That does not establish all upstream code, dataset or weight redistribution rights. The linked dataset record dates to July 15, 2025, despite the journal article being published in 2026; its description still references a 2025 paper. Do not call the dataset a new October release. No package, data or weights were downloaded. Pinned license, dataset record

2. Japanese binocular modeling: a hypothesis to test independently

VISAPP 2026; exact publication day not established. Nagoya Institute of Technology's Yingxin, Sakaue and Sato describe a shared display image optimized through separate eye models and a fusion/rivalry surrogate. Their binocular evaluation uses 1,000 training and 200 test synthetic combinations. This is simulation evidence, not a measured benefit for people reading an ordinary screen. It is relevant because VIEW4REAL's current weighted two-eye objective deliberately does not model binocular fusion. Publisher record, paper

Decision: consider only an isolated comparison of the fusion surrogate against the existing per-eye metrics. Never allow a better fused score to conceal worsening either eye. The paper is labeled CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; no reusable implementation or model license was verified. Do not import its figures, code or weights into a commercial app on the assumption that an accessible paper grants those rights. Browser/Windows runtime and compute requirements were not established.

3. Gaze-adaptive near-eye correction: useful calibration evidence, different hardware

Array volume 29, March 2026; reviewed October 16. Jiang et al. use calibrated spatially varying PSFs and gaze-aware neural processing, with simulation and a VR optical-system/camera bench. Reported inference is approximately 11 ms for a 280 × 280 region on an RTX 4090. That is not a full ordinary-screen latency result. The suggested approximately 1 ms optimized deployment should not be represented as the reported measured baseline. Data are available on request; a redistributable implementation/weights package was not verified. Official paper

Decision: retain as support for measured, gaze-dependent forward-model validation. It does not justify adding eye tracking, a neural dependency, or a vision-correction claim to the current Windows app. No camera permission or eye-data collection is proposed.

4. Binocular motion safety belongs in later validation

Published May 4, 2026. A 53-participant study found that interocular blur/light differences can alter processing delays and produce visible motion-depth illusions for some participants. Its apparatus was an iPad modified with a lenticular sheet, trial lenses and head stabilization. It does not test VIEW4REAL. Primary study

Decision: keep future motion, discomfort and per-eye endpoints separate from static image metrics. Do not infer safe binocular daily use from a static simulated MSE improvement. Formal human evaluation needs appropriate professional/ethics guidance and explicit informed participation.

Practical app verification candidates

MAX-ALPHA: small provider-contract check with a verified source target

Google's migration guide, updated October 13, 2026, says Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite ignores deprecated sampling settings and rejects non-empty final model-role prefills. It also recommends removing unsupported candidate_count for Gemini 3+. Official guide

The current Gemini worker source sends temperature: 0, candidateCount: 1 and maxOutputTokens: 512; its last content turn is already correctly user. This establishes a compatibility-cleanup candidate, not a demonstrated runtime failure. Recommended later scope: a no-network fixture check for supported request fields and complete, blocked, empty, truncated, cancelled and failed responses. Preserve the configured model, single-question boundary, authentication, no-history payload, no automatic retries/fallback and opt-in behavior. No account, SDK, model or security change is needed for the fixture audit. A health response still does not prove inference.

StatusPing: qualify the exact downloadable APK

Android 15 cancels PendingIntents after Force Stop; reopening provides an opportunity to register future work. Alarm permission revocation and reboot also require explicit handling. These are existing platform behaviors; the documentation updates dated October 1, 2026 are not new feature-release dates. Stopped-state behavior, alarm scheduling

Prepare acceptance cases tied to the APK hash, signing identity and matching source: screen-off/Doze, reboot, force-stop then reopen, notification/alarm permissions denied and restored, timezone/DST changes and duplicate prevention. Use synthetic local reminders. Do not expand permissions or switch scheduling modes in this review. A local reminder is not a delivered family alert. Real-phone evidence remains distinct from Android 35 source CI.

Questrix, The Commissary and BOARDROOM: verify save and restore

WebKit describes best-effort eviction, quota failure and heuristic persistence grants. The policy article is from August 10, 2023, not this week's release; the Storage standard is a living specification. Persistence does not establish backup. WebKit policy, Storage standard

First identify whether each deployed record is browser-held or Express-backed. Then use synthetic Questrix responses, Commissary stock movements and BOARDROOM chats to verify interrupted writes, restart, concurrent changes and isolated export/restore. Check record counts, relationships and totals, and never show “saved” after a failed write. Test Safari tab and Home Screen instances separately without deleting existing data. Preserve owner-only access and existing mail-status semantics. No new persistence permission, service or OCR engine is selected.

StockPilot: keep sandbox, restore and production claims separate

Undated living documentation; reviewed October 16, 2026. eBay Sandbox supports fictional transactions but has documented gaps, including shipping-label behavior, dispute transitions and production-equivalent fees. Sandbox guide, limitations

Prepare an endpoint-specific matrix using synthetic identities: request/read-back identifier reconciliation, timeout/duplicate behavior, unsupported-flow labels, and an isolated restore preserving inventory, order mappings, pending jobs and audit history. Restoring must not trigger live fulfillment. The existing 284 mocked tests do not supply this evidence. No new keys, live orders, supplier requests or account setup occurred.

Recommended next tests and acceptance baseline

Ordinary screens provide a bounded, nonnegative shared image; they cannot emit negative radiance or recover frequencies completely lost by the optical transfer function. Independent eye images require separate optical channels. A sphere/cylinder/axis prescription alone does not measure pupil, accommodation, higher-order aberrations, screen spectrum or binocular perception. The current model interprets inputs as corneal-plane-equivalent powers, without spectacle vertex-distance conversion or worn correction. Keep those assumptions explicit rather than presenting prescription entry as calibration. These are retained constraints from the project's optical model, not a new clinical finding.

  1. Smallest actionable engineering candidate: MAX-ALPHA's mocked Gemini request-contract check. Passing source tests would still not establish deployed-worker inference.
  2. VIEW4REAL: prioritize its already-documented missing optical-bench/native validation over adding another model. A subsequent comparison can use synthetic PSFs and newly generated text/patterns, including Latin/CJK text, at fixed calibrated scale. Keep normal rendering, enlargement/accessibility and simple sharpening baselines; do not rank enlarged content against an inequivalent small-text target.
  3. Preserve fixed linear-light/radiance conventions, bounded pixels, clipping rates, independent PSF holdouts, distance/pupil/accommodation mismatch, and separate-eye results. Record runtime, memory and cancellation on actual supported hardware. Keep accessible semantic controls, zoom/reflow and an immediate neutral reset; a rendered image must not replace the usable text interface.
  4. Keep prescriptions, eye measurements, camera feeds and personal documents out of benchmark fixtures and default exports. Validate exact code/data/model licenses before bundling. No newly reviewed evidence establishes commercial readiness, eyesight correction, or replacement for prescribed eyewear.

The current validation record already retains a mismatch case where inverse processing worsens one eye and another case where sharpening performs better. Those negative results should remain visible in future comparisons.
