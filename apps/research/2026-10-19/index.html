Older human study: adaptation is not captured by blur alone

Newly checked here; published September 6, 2025. Son and colleagues studied 42 adults using orientation judgments and EEG, comparing chronic astigmatism with short-term lens-induced astigmatism under controlled monocular conditions. Their results show that similar optical distortion need not imply the same perception after long-term adaptation. This is human mechanistic evidence, not a display-precompensation, binocular-reading or clinical-benefit trial. Inference for future evaluation: do not treat simulated retinal images or acutely induced blur as interchangeable with prescription-matched users. The article is CC BY-NC; separate rights for linked analysis/data deposits were not verified. No assets were incorporated. Primary paper

Older Chinese optical-bench study: spectrum and real optics matter

Zhao and colleagues' “Development and Experimental Study of Visual Analysis System for All Aberration Correction of Human Eye” appeared online August 4, 2025, in the November 2025 issue. Those dates come from the author-uploaded paper; the host's January metadata is misleading. Ten young adults were tested with a physical adaptive-optics/Badal apparatus, controlled right-eye accommodation and color-dependent contrast tasks. This is human optical-bench evidence, not a shared-screen software or binocular-reading result. It supports retaining spectrum, channel luminance and real-eye validation as separate considerations; it does not validate View4Real. No deployable runtime, reusable code/weights or redistribution license was established. Author-uploaded primary paper, DOI 10.3788/LOP251678

Japanese search and software-release dating

The Sato–Sakaue laboratory's past-research page describes pre-deconvolution and special light-field displays. It does not establish a new October 2026 controlled ordinary-screen result. Earlier hardware-dependent or simulated results remain separate from commercial readiness.

The PyOLimp default-branch head checked today is an April 27, 2026 README-link fix; its GitHub releases list was empty. This does not exclude distribution elsewhere. The known September 20 paper must not be relabeled a September software release.

Decision: retain semantic text, original-image bypass, enlargement and ordinary sharpening as baselines. An ordinary shared screen has bounded nonnegative light output and cannot independently address both eyes; prescription values alone do not calibrate pupil, accommodation, display spectrum or perception. Future original Latin/CJK synthetic comparisons should retain per-eye mismatch and negative cases, fixed physical scale, contrast/clipping/halos, latency, memory and cancellation. The previously recorded neural runtimes are not live-display rates. Independent optical-bench validation and appropriate controlled reading-accuracy, speed and comfort evidence remain missing. No prescription, eye image, camera feed, personal document, model or dataset upload is needed for synthetic work. No eyesight improvement or eyewear replacement is claimed.

Accessibility: evaluate complete workflows

Newly checked here; published July 23, 2026. WCAG-EM 2.0 is an informative W3C Group Note covering digital products, including native, hybrid and cross-platform applications. It adds no WCAG requirements and does not itself establish conformance. Its practical contribution is to define the product scope, browser/assistive-technology baseline, representative views and complete processes, then record findings. A sampled audit does not justify an entire-product conformance claim.

Decision: future checks should identify exact app/package versions and complete sign-in, reading, survey, inventory, reminder and export/restore flows, including error, dialog and interrupted states. Reuse established WCAG 2.2 checks rather than treating the September 10, 2026 WCAG 3.0 Working Draft as a final replacement standard. No accessibility conformance or new device test is claimed.
